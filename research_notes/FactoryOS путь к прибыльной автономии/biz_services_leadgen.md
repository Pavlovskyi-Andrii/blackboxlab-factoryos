## 1. Market pricing for AI automation agencies and productized AI services

### Takeaway
Published 2026 price lists cluster at USD 1.5-8k setup plus USD 300-1,500/month for small-business work, but they are agency blogs describing what they wish to charge. No source gives audited deal sizes or retention. What is consistently sold is one workflow outcome, not "AI agents".

### Cited findings
- Setup fees quoted across 2026 guides: USD 1,500-5,000; USD 2,000-8,000; USD 1,500-25,000 upfront. Monthly retainers: USD 300-1,500; USD 1,200-7,500; USD 50-2,000 "to keep it running". All vendor/agency blogs - [Layer3 Labs, updated 2 Jul 2026](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-agency-pricing); [Taskip "USD 500-20K+ and 6 models"](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/); [Ciela, 18 Mar 2026](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-strategy); [SoluLab](https://www.solulab.com/ai-automation-cost-for-small-businesses/); [dev.to operator guide](https://dev.to/scalelogix_ai/how-to-price-ai-agency-services-in-2026-a-complete-guide-for-operators-5f26). The search extract did not map each range to one page; treat the ranges as the envelope.
- Productized packages: "usually USD 1,800-4,500/month" per one guide and "USD 3-15k/month" per another - [Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/); [DevShopVault](https://www.devshopvault.com/library/the-6-ways-agencies-productize-ai-services).
- Advice repeated in the guides: price against the payroll cost replaced, and small businesses should ask for pricing around one workflow outcome; some vendors show a low setup fee and load the retainer, so compare first-year totals - same guides as above.
- Margin anecdote (ANECDOTAL, second-hand): an r/agency thread described a white-label AI receptionist resold at USD 255/month against about USD 55/month cost (about 78% gross margin) - [AllAble](https://www.allable.ai/blog/ai-automation-agency/). The same article notes that sellers "show revenue screenshots, not retention numbers".
- Failure context: Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 (cost, unclear value, weak risk controls) and estimates only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real ("agent washing") - [Gartner press release, 25 Jun 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027). Gartner also predicts 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward-deployed engineering by 2028 - [Gartner, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-29-gartner-predicts-70-percent-of-enterprises-will-abandon-agentic-ai-built-by-vendor-forward-deployed-engineering-by-2028). Only 17% of organisations have deployed AI agents; over 60% expect to within two years - [Gartner article](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/ai-agent-reality-check).
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (Aug 2025): 95% of enterprise gen-AI pilots stall before scaled adoption; externally bought tools succeed about two-thirds of the time versus one-third for internal builds (150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 deployments) - [Fortune, 18 Aug 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/). Enterprise sample, not SMB.
- S&P Global figure quoted second-hand: 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% - [AllAble](https://www.allable.ai/blog/ai-automation-agency/) (not checked against S&P).
- Churn baselines quoted without sources: SMB software 2-4% monthly; agencies 15-20% annually; "high-churn" AI receptionist agencies about 10% monthly - [AllAble](https://www.allable.ai/blog/ai-automation-agency/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/blog/agencies-reduce-client-churn-ai/); [MyVoiceAIConnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/reduce-ai-receptionist-client-churn-rate). Vendor claims.
- One vendor survey claim: 36.6% of agencies say more than a quarter of their clients arrived after a failed prior project - [The SaaS Library](https://thesaaslibrary.com/why-do-ai-agents-fail-second-attempt-buyers/). Unverified, but it points to "rescue/fix" as a buyer trigger.

### Inferences
- A newcomer with zero case studies will not get the listed USD 2-8k setup fees. Realistic first deals are paid pilots of USD 300-1,000 for one workflow, or marketplace fixed-price jobs of USD 100-800.
- The "outside tool succeeds twice as often as an internal build" finding (MIT) is the best available argument for an external specialist - but it is enterprise data.
- "Rescue of a failed AI/automation project" and "fix my vibe-coded app" are stronger entry triggers than "let us add AI agents".

### Gaps
- No audited data on AI-agency deal sizes, churn or delivery failure rates was found. No real founder post-mortem with numbers surfaced. The planned search on small-business AI adoption surveys (US Census BTOS, US Chamber) did not run.

### Inferences - CAC ranges (assumptions stated; these are my estimates, not sourced numbers)
- **Upwork bidding.** Assume USD 1-3 per proposal and a 1-3% win rate for a new profile with no reviews (below the 3-7% "healthy" benchmark). That is 33-100 proposals, i.e. about **USD 50-200 cash plus 10-25 hours per first contract**, then about 10% platform fee. 10 free Connects cover roughly one proposal a month, so bidding is effectively closed under a literal "zero additional spend" rule; it needs an owner-approved USD 20-100.
- **Cold e-mail by hand, owner-approved.** At 3.4% replies and 0.3-1.2% meetings per contact, 900 approved contacts in a month (30/day) give about 3-11 meetings and, at an assumed 10% close for an unknown solo vendor, roughly **0-1 customers per month, at 60-100 founder hours**. Scaling needs extra domains, mailboxes, data and a sender (an assumed USD 100-250+/month, not verified here) - i.e. new paid infrastructure.
- **LinkedIn by hand.** 28.5% acceptance x 10.4% reply is about 3 conversations per 100 invitations; at an assumed 25% positive share that is under 1 meeting per 100 invitations. Slow unless targeting is event-driven (someone just launched an app).
- **Warm network / referrals / partner agencies.** Cash CAC about zero and usually the highest conversion, but the founder has no audience; no benchmark found.
- Marketplaces double as the billing rail: Stripe is unavailable, and software-only merchants of record cannot be used for audit/consulting work.

## 3. AI SDR / AI lead generation as a service

### Takeaway
This is the category with the most public disappointment and the least fit: prices are high because the product is sold on a promise, measured reply rates are falling, AI-written messages underperform human ones, and delivery requires paid sending infrastructure plus compliance work. A founder who has not yet generated leads for himself has nothing to prove the service works.

### Cited findings

Twelve checks a month are needed for about USD 1,000 net. On its own this is a low-wage job, not a company.
- **Buyer.** Hobbyists will use the free platform scan. The paying segment is a vibe-coded app with real users, payments or a B2B customer asking security questions, and small agencies that ship vibe-coded apps for clients (reseller/partner route).
- **Why demand persists despite free scanners.** The Nov 2026 scan suggests owners do not run or do not act on free scans. The trigger is an event (launch, first enterprise prospect, public incident), so outreach should be event-timed and opt-in.

(2) List one fixed-scope offer on marketplace catalogues, where buyers search "vibe coding" and "Claude Code". (3) Offer 5-10 free opt-in checks in build-in-public communities in exchange for a testimonial and permission to publish anonymised findings. (4) Convert every free check with findings into a fixed-price fix quote. (5) Approach 10-20 small "vibe-coding agencies" for a white-label per-app check.
- **Liability.** A "no critical findings" report can be read as assurance. Keep the kit's wording ("not a pentest, no guarantee"), written scope and consent before touching anything, a liability cap equal to the fee, and no testing of live systems without authorisation. Client code sent to consumer LLM subscriptions needs the explicit opt-in the kit already requires.
- **Naming risk.** "BlackBox Vision" already sells a "Vibe Coding Audit for AI-Built Apps" at USD 250; "Blackbox Lab AI Security Check" is confusingly close.
- **Tooling.** Use licence-clean scanners (Opengrep, gitleaks, pip-audit, bandit - each still needs the licence check the kit lists) rather than Semgrep-maintained rule packs in a paid service.

## Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Growth percentages are not market size.** Upwork and Fiverr report growth of searches or earnings from small bases; neither shows how many small jobs a new, unreviewed seller actually wins. Upwork itself says low-complexity AI work is growing while its pay falls.
- **Benchmarks come from sellers.** Instantly, Expandi, GigRadar, SmartReach, lemlist and the lead-gen agencies all profit from the activity they measure, and their definitions differ by up to 8x (0.45% vs 3.43% vs 10% "reply rate").
- **Security statistics are produced by security vendors.** Veracode, Wiz and Escape sell security. The trend is also improving (pass rate 56% -> 62% in 2026), and platforms are bundling scanners, which shrinks the paid niche over time.
- **Incidents do not prove willingness to pay.** No source shows conversion from "my app is exposed" to a paid audit. The crowd of USD 149-499 audit pages shows many sellers, not many buyers.
- **Agency price lists are aspirations.** No retention or deal-size data was found; the one margin figure is a Reddit anecdote retold by a blog.
- **AI SDR evidence is old at the core.** The solid reporting (TechCrunch) is from March 2025; 2026 churn numbers come from competitors.
- **Legal statements are from vendor blogs,** not statutes or counsel (German UWG, Stripe eligibility, licences).
- **Enterprise studies (MIT, Gartner) may not transfer to SMBs.**
- **My CAC figures are arithmetic on those benchmarks plus assumptions** (win rate for a new profile, close rate, tooling costs). They could be off by a factor of two or more in either direction.
- **This session could not open any page**, so numbers may carry summarisation errors; re-check anything quoted in the final report.

## Implications for FactoryOS (opinionated)

1. **Nobody will pay for "an autonomous AI company". They pay for a fixed thing: a working workflow, a fixed app, a list of what to fix.** The platform is a cost advantage in delivery, not the product. Sell the outcome; do not mention agents in the headline.
2. **Run one funnel, not four products.** Marketplace (M1) is the channel; "launch security check + fix for AI-built apps" (M2) is the entry offer; ongoing engineering (M5) is the upsell to the same clients. Park the validation lab (M6) and AI-SDR-as-a-service (M4).
3. **Re-price the security check.** Keep USD 99-149 only as a credit toward a fix sprint, or make the check free and opt-in. Sell the fix (USD 300-1,500) and a re-check subscription. A USD 99 one-off cannot fund the company (about USD 83 net and 1.5-2 human hours each).
4. **"Zero additional spend" blocks the fastest channel.** Ten free Connects is about one proposal a month. The single spend worth asking the owner for is USD 20-100 of Connects for a 30-day test - cheaper than any outbound stack and with payment collection included. If refused, the only free routes are catalogue listings and community opt-ins, and the timeline to first payment roughly doubles.
5. **Do not build an outbound machine.** At benchmark rates, owner-approved manual outreach yields roughly 0-1 customers per month for 60-100 hours of work; automating it needs paid domains/mailboxes and conflicts with the approval gate and German opt-in law. Use the agents to research and draft, keep volumes tiny and event-targeted.
6. **Payment and legal plumbing decide the channel.** Stripe is not available, software merchants of record exclude services, so marketplaces or direct invoices through an FOP are the practical options; taxes are about 6% of receipts plus about 1,900 UAH a month.
7. **Fix the pilot kit's open gates before any sale:** licence-clean scanners (Opengrep rather than Semgrep-maintained rules), written scope and consent, liability cap, explicit opt-in before client code touches consumer AI subscriptions, and a name that does not collide with BlackBox Vision's existing USD 250 audit.
8. **Expected first 60 days if executed:** 1-3 small contracts, USD 100-1,500 of revenue, close to zero net profit after fees and the founder's hours. That is a validation result, not a failure; the kill criteria in the sheet above tell when to stop.
9. **The data that would change this verdict:** a marketplace reply rate under 2.5% on 40 proposals (channel dead for this profile); or 10 free checks producing zero paid follow-ups (offer dead); or, the other way, one client accepting a monthly engineering retainer (then M5 becomes the company).
